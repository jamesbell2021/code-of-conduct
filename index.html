01 — Why this exists

The short version

This page is a plain-English guide to Belfast Met's Student Code of Conduct — the actual policy is a short but formal document, and this walks through what it means for you day to day. Where this page and the real policy differ, the real policy wins.

What the College is trying to do

Belfast Met wants campus — and every place learning happens, from a classroom to a placement to an online session — to be safe, inclusive and welcoming for everyone in it: students, staff, visitors, neighbours, and the employers who take students on placement. The Code exists to protect that, and to protect the College's own reputation from students who don't hold up their end.

Who it applies to, and when

Every Belfast Met student, from the moment you enrol until you finish your course. It doesn't switch off outside the building. The Code applies:

On campus

Any Belfast Met site, any time.

At College events off campus

Trips, competitions, socials, anything run by the College elsewhere.

On placement or at work

You're representing the College there too.

In your accommodation, and online

Including student housing and remote/online classes.

The one thing worth remembering

This isn't really a list of rules to memorise. It's what "being a decent person other people can rely on" looks like, written down so there's no ambiguity about where the line is — and what happens if you cross it.

02 — Your obligations

Learning the Met Way

Belfast Met wants every student to live out four values — Collaboration, Ambition, Respect and Excellence, or "CARE". They're the spirit behind everything on this page.

The specifics

    03 — Misconduct

    What counts as breaking it

    The policy splits misconduct into ten categories. None of these lists are exhaustive — behaving badly in a way that isn't spelled out here can still get you in trouble. Tap each one.

    If something's happened to you

    If you've experienced anything covered by Abusive or Sexual misconduct above — from someone else, whether they're a student or not — that's not something you have to sort out alone. Talk to James, contact Safeguarding at safeguarding@belfastmet.ac.uk, or reach Student Counselling via Inspire — confidential, 24/7, all year: 0808 800 0032 or inspiresupporthub.org/students (log-on BMETHUB). You don't need to be in crisis, and you don't need a referral.

    04 — Consequences

    What happens if you don't

    Breaking the Code isn't dealt with by this page — it goes through the College's Student Disciplinary Policy. Here's the shape of it.

    1. A breach is alleged Someone reports it, or staff observe it directly.
    2. It's investigated Under the Student Disciplinary Policy, not made up on the spot.
    3. If it's proven, action follows What happens depends on how serious it is and whether it's happened before.
    4. Serious or repeated breaches can end your place Dismissal from the College is a real possible outcome, not an idle threat.

    This list isn't exhaustive

    The ten categories in section 03 are examples, not the complete set. Something can still count as misconduct even if it isn't written down word for word.

    If you disagree with an outcome

    Talk to James first. The disciplinary process itself has its own right of appeal, and you'll be told how to use it if it comes to that.

    Complaints

    Unhappy about how the College has handled something? Raise it through the College's own complaints procedure first — that's what it's there for, and most things get sorted through it.

    Still not satisfied once you've been through that? You can take it to the Northern Ireland Public Services Ombudsman (NIPSO), who act as an independent referee — but they'll normally expect you to have used the College's process first.

    Northern Ireland Public Services Ombudsman, Progressive House, 33 Wellington Place, Belfast, BT1 6HN.
